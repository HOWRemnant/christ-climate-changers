Programme Vision

Not just knowledge.
Transformation.

This programme is designed to develop spiritually mature, practically equipped leaders prepared for ministry and marketplace impact. It is not merely academic — it is a journey of character refinement, skill development, and Kingdom deployment.

The Discipleship for Leadership Masters' Training Course combines Biblical foundations with hands-on practical ministry experience, discipleship accountability, and strategic leadership development — producing leaders capable of influencing the church, the marketplace, and society.

"Leadership is a calling, not a career. May you be shaped into a leader after God's own heart, ready to serve, build, and thrive."

Course Structure

Two Years. Six Modules.
One Transformational Journey.

The programme is divided into two years: Year 1 focuses on Foundation and Formation; Year 2 on Application and Activation. Each module concludes with a formal assessment and commissioning before progression.

Module 01
Character & Spiritual Formation
The inner life of the leader before public ministry

Leaders are formed before they are deployed. This module focuses on the internal architecture of Kingdom leadership — character, consecration, accountability, and the awareness of blind spots that can derail even gifted leaders.

  • The Call to Leadership — divine invitation and personal responsibility Luke 22:31-32
  • Self-Reflection & Personal Accountability — the discipline of honest self-assessment 2 Cor 13:5
  • Identifying Blind Spots in Leadership — what we cannot see about ourselves Matt 7:3-5
  • Character vs Reputation — what God sees vs what others see 1 Sam 16:7
  • Developing a Lifestyle of Consecration & Worship Romans 12:1-2
  • Leadership Discipleship — the locker room where leaders are truly formed Titus 1:7-9

Learning Through Example

Biblical & Living Models of Leadership

Each module draws from both Scripture and history — showing that the principles taught here have been lived, tested, and proven across every generation.

Biblical Example
Peter
Luke 22:31-32 — From impulse to apostolic authority

Peter's transformation from an impulsive disciple to a bold apostle illustrates how character refinement and spiritual formation shape a leader. Jesus prayed for him, knowing that his faith would be tested — and that through the test he would emerge ready to strengthen others.

Living Example
Billy Graham
1918–2018 — Integrity, discipline, and lifelong consistency

Graham's decades of ministry were marked by financial accountability, personal holiness, and unwavering commitment to his calling. He surrounded himself with accountability, avoided compromise, and demonstrated that spiritual formation is not a season — it is a lifestyle.

Biblical Example
Moses
Exodus 18:13-26 — Delegation and structure in leadership

Moses initially managed all leadership responsibilities alone — until Jethro provided the wisdom of delegation and team structure. His willingness to receive counsel and restructure his leadership is a masterclass in building sustainable ministry teams.

Living Example
Bishop T.D. Jakes
1957–Present — Multi-generational, multi-cultural church leadership

Jakes has built a church that crosses cultural, generational, and social boundaries — while consistently investing in and empowering the next generation of leaders. His model of discipleship within the house of God reflects the principles of Module 2.

Biblical Example
Paul
2 Timothy 2:2; 1 Corinthians 2:4-5 — Teaching, training, and equipping

Paul's ministry combined powerful preaching with intentional pastoral care and deliberate discipleship. He discipled Timothy and Titus, ensuring that sound doctrine, pastoral integrity, and practical ministry competence were passed on to the next generation.

Living Example
Reinhard Bonnke
1940–2019 — Large-scale evangelism and ministry development

Bonnke dedicated his life to preaching the Gospel with practical power, training African leaders, and conducting large-scale ministry events that reached tens of millions. His commitment to both proclamation and equipping defines effective practical ministry.

Biblical Example
Joseph
Genesis 41:39-44 — Kingdom leadership in secular structures

Joseph governed Egypt — one of the most powerful nations in the ancient world — while maintaining his covenant identity, integrity, and dependence on God. He never separated his faith from his professional responsibilities. He demonstrates what Kingdom marketplace leadership looks like at the highest level.

Living Example
Truett Cathy
1921–2014 — Founder of Chick-fil-A

Cathy built one of America's most successful fast-food companies on the foundation of Christian values — including closing on Sundays, servant leadership, and investing in young people. He proved that faithfulness and fruitfulness are not in conflict in the marketplace.

Biblical Example
Elijah & Elisha
2 Kings 2:9-10 — Intentional succession and the transfer of mantle

Elijah's discipleship of Elisha is one of Scripture's most powerful models of succession. Rather than holding on, Elijah transferred what he carried — and Elisha received a double portion. This module teaches how to hold your leadership with an open hand and prepare the next generation well.

Living Example
John C. Maxwell
1947–Present — Succession, deployment, and next-generation training

Maxwell has spent decades not just leading but creating leaders — writing, teaching, and building organisations specifically designed to develop and release the next generation. His emphasis on intentional succession and multiplication models the heart of Module 5.

Biblical Example
Nehemiah
Nehemiah 2:17-18; 6:15-16 — Strategic planning, assessment, and completion

Nehemiah assessed the ruins of Jerusalem, developed a strategic plan, mobilised a team, overcame opposition, and completed the wall in 52 days. He documented the work, reviewed progress, and celebrated the outcome. His leadership is a template for reflective, strategic, and mission-driven completion.

Living Example
Rick Warren
1954–Present — Visionary planning and documented strategy

Warren's ability to document, reflect, and implement sustainable leadership strategy has shaped the thinking of church leaders worldwide. His emphasis on purposeful, blueprint-driven leadership mirrors the reflective and strategic demands of the final portfolio module.
